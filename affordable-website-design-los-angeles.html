Website Buyer’s Guide

Affordable website design in Los Angeles: what should you actually compare?

Published July 30, 2026 · Galaxy Gleam Media

The cheapest quote is not always affordable after missed leads, surprise fees and a rebuild. A useful comparison focuses on what the website must accomplish.

1. Mobile quality

Most prospects will encounter the business on a phone. Navigation, contact buttons, text size and page speed should work without pinching or hunting.

2. A clear offer and next step

A visually impressive site can still fail if visitors cannot understand what the company does, who it helps and how to get started. Every important page needs one primary action.

3. SEO foundations

At minimum, the build should include useful page titles and descriptions, one clear main heading, crawlable text, canonical URLs, a sitemap and pages targeted to the services customers search.

4. Ownership and ongoing costs

Ask who controls the domain, hosting and content, what recurring fees apply, and what happens if you change providers. Get those answers before launch.

5. Proof that matches your project

Review real examples for mobile layout, clarity and range. A portfolio should help you assess the provider’s design judgment—not promise that your results will match another business.

Galaxy Gleam approach: Custom business websites start at $400, typical delivery is 1–2 days for a prepared small-business project, and optional AI chatbots are available. Final timing and price depend on scope and content readiness.

Questions to ask before hiring

  • What exact pages and revisions are included?
  • Who supplies the copy and images?
  • Will analytics and Search Console be connected?
  • Are hosting or platform costs separate?
  • What happens after the site launches?
