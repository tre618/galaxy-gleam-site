Your Owned Presence

Give prospective clients a reason to choose you specifically

Portal profiles and social platforms can help people discover agents, but they also place competitors close by and control how your information is presented. Your website gives you a place to explain your approach, market focus and services in your own voice.

Personal positioning

State the areas, property types and client needs you genuinely serve. Avoid generic claims that could describe every agent in Los Angeles.

Buyer and seller paths

Separate the questions, concerns and next steps for buyers, sellers, investors or renters rather than sending everyone to the same vague page.

Direct lead capture

Use clear calls, inquiry forms or booking links so prospects can contact you without being routed through a directory.

Core Website Pages

Organize the site around real client decisions

Home and agent story

Explain who you help, where you work and what makes your process different. Use an accurate biography, professional photo and required brokerage information.

Buyer services

Answer questions about consultation, search preparation, offers and the next step without making promises about outcomes.

Seller services

Describe how you prepare, market and communicate about a listing using only services and experience you can support.

Markets and neighborhoods

Create useful, original pages for areas you actually serve. Thin pages that only swap a city name are not a sound local SEO strategy.

IDX Planning

Decide whether searchable listings belong in the first build

IDX can be valuable when property search is central to the website strategy, but it adds vendor, data, eligibility, design and maintenance considerations. It should not be added simply because every other agent has it.

A focused brand site may be enough when

  • Your immediate need is professional credibility.
  • Most leads come from referrals or direct relationships.
  • You want dedicated buyer, seller or neighborhood content.
  • You already direct property searches through another approved system.

Evaluate IDX when

  • Searchable listings are a major part of the customer journey.
  • You meet the applicable MLS and vendor requirements.
  • You have budget for setup and ongoing provider costs.
  • You have a plan to promote and maintain the experience.
Scope note: IDX is not included automatically. Eligibility, data access, third-party fees and technical requirements must be confirmed before it is quoted.

Local SEO Foundations

Publish real expertise instead of interchangeable location pages

Search visibility grows from useful, accurate pages that match the services and communities you actually know. A strong neighborhood page should help a potential client understand the area or transaction—not repeat the same paragraph with a new ZIP code.

Technical foundations can include unique titles and descriptions, logical headings, internal links, mobile-friendly structure and appropriate structured data. Frequently Asked Questions

Real estate website design questions

What pages should an agent website include?

A focused site commonly includes a clear homepage, about page, buyer and seller service pages, contact information, accurate market or neighborhood content and any required brokerage or licensing disclosures.

Does every agent website need IDX?

No. IDX can be useful when searchable listings are central to the strategy, but some agents are better served by a focused brand, service and lead-capture website. Requirements and ongoing costs should be confirmed first.

Can the website guarantee leads?

No. A website can improve credibility, content ownership and the path to contact, but traffic and leads depend on promotion, search visibility, the offer, competition and follow-up.

Can you redesign an existing agent website?

Yes. The first step is reviewing the current pages, mobile experience, content, forms, domain setup and any existing search visibility. Use our website redesign checklist to prepare.
