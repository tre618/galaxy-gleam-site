The Mobile Decision

What restaurant customers usually need first

A restaurant visitor may be checking the site from a sidewalk, car or group chat. The design should prioritize quick decisions instead of forcing people through decorative pages before they can find basic information.

A readable menu

Menu content should be easy to scan on a phone. When possible, key menu information should appear as readable page content instead of only as a tiny image or difficult PDF.

Current hours and location

Show accurate hours, address, service area when relevant, phone number and a direct directions link. Keep this information consistent with business listings.

One clear primary action

Make the preferred action obvious: reserve a table, order online, call, visit or request catering. Too many competing buttons can slow the decision.

Restaurant Website Features

Build the site around the way your business serves customers

Dining and reservations

Present the atmosphere, menu and location clearly, then connect visitors to the reservation system you already use. Custom integrations are scoped separately when needed.

Takeout and delivery

Direct customers toward the preferred ordering platform and explain pickup, delivery or direct-order options without making them hunt across several pages.

Catering and private events

Create a dedicated inquiry path with service details, capacity or package information you provide, and a focused contact form.

Food trucks and pop-ups

Highlight the current schedule, service area, booking information and social channels so customers and event organizers know where to find you.

Local Search Foundations

Give search engines consistent, useful restaurant information

A website supports local discovery when its business name, address, phone, hours and cuisine or service descriptions are accurate and consistent. Clear location content, descriptive titles, internal links, image alternative text and appropriate structured data help search engines understand the page.

The website should support—not contradict—your Google Business Profile, Yelp profile, reservation account and ordering platforms. No designer can guarantee a first-page ranking, but clean technical foundations and useful content give the restaurant a stronger base for ongoing Los Angeles SEO work.

Content Checklist

  • Parking, accessibility or service notes you want published

Customer actions

  • Reservation link

  • Catering inquiry details

Brand assets

  • Logo and approved colors
  • Food and interior photos you can publish
  • Current menu and prices

Ownership and updates

  • Who approves the website

  • Domain and platform access
  • Preferred maintenance plan

Common Problems

Restaurant website mistakes that cost attention

  • Uploading a menu that is unreadable on a phone.
  • Showing different hours on the website and business listings.
  • Using large images that make the page slow on a mobile connection.
  • Hiding the address, phone number or reservation link.
  • Sending every visitor to a social profile instead of providing dependable website information.
  • Leaving expired events, old prices or broken third-party links live.

Frequently Asked Questions

Restaurant web design questions

What should a restaurant website include?

At minimum, make the menu, current hours, address, directions, phone number and primary reservation or ordering option easy to find on a phone.

Can you connect our ordering or reservation service?

Existing third-party ordering and reservation links can usually be added. Custom integrations depend on the provider, account access and project scope.

Can the website help with Google visibility?

Yes, the site can support local visibility with accurate information, readable location and menu content, descriptive titles, internal links and appropriate structured data. Rankings are not guaranteed.
